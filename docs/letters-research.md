# Letter and correspondence management worldwide: research report

Prepared for the Letters module of EUISIS (Agelgay), 2026-11-09. Owner's
question: what do governments and standards bodies do about official letters,
and what should our module take from it.

## 1. Method and limits

Twenty-six web searches on 2026-11-09 covering: the international records
standards (ISO, MoReq, ICA, national archives); the national correspondence or
e-office systems of fifteen countries and the European Commission; public
service standards for answering letters; authenticity checks on printed
letters; electronic signature law; and the Ethiopian situation. Official
documents (standards bodies, ministries, archives, system manuals) are the
basis of every finding; vendor pages were used only to confirm which features
are commonplace, never as authority. Where a source is old, undated or
second-hand, the report says so. Two things could not be found: a published
Ethiopian correspondence standard, and any public description of an Ethiopian
government letter system.

## 2. Findings in brief

1. Every mature government runs **one registry per institution with two
   books**: an inward register (letters received, numbered on receipt) and a
   dispatch register (letters sent, numbered on dispatch). The registry
   number is unique within the book, assigned once, never changed.
2. **Registration is a records-management act** (ISO 15489): capture the
   record, give it a unique identifier, classify it, set its access level,
   then track every use. The metadata captured at registration is the
   skeleton of everything later.
3. **Movement with instructions** is the heart of handling: the head reads the
   letter, writes a disposition (ትእዛዝ, "noting", "disposisi") to a unit or an
   officer with a deadline, and the system records who held the letter when.
4. **Deadlines are policy, enforced by the system**: public service charters
   set reply targets (5 to 20 working days), systems chase the holder before
   and after the date, and an interim reply is expected when a deadline will
   slip.
5. **Letters between institutions travel inside the system** where both
   institutions use it (Austria since 2004, Estonia's DHX, Russia's MEDO,
   Indonesia's SRIKANDI, Saudi Morasalat); the receiving registry still
   assigns its own inward number.
6. **Authenticity of the paper copy** is answered with a verification code or
   QR code on the letter and a public check that reveals nothing more than
   issuer, number and date (Luxembourg GouvCheck, India, Malaysia).
7. **Electronic signature** replaces the wet signature only where the law and
   a national certification authority exist (eIDAS qualified signatures in
   the EU, Turkey's KEP, Indonesia's BSrE). Elsewhere the system records the
   approval and the paper is still signed.
8. **Classification and access** are explicit: a file or subject code from an
   approved scheme, and a security level that limits who reads the contents
   while the register row stays visible on a need-to-know basis.
9. **Retention and disposal** come from the national archives as approved
   schedules; Malaysia will not even switch an agency on until its file
   classification and disposal schedule are approved.
10. **Scale is proven**: India's eOffice runs in 790 organisations with over a
    million users and 248 million receipts; Korea's On-nara has been
    mandatory for all central agencies since 2007; Austria's ELAK replaced
    paper files in every federal ministry in 2004.

complexity, and an interim reply when the deadline will slip. For Ethiopia the
standard would come from the Civil Service proclamation and each institution's
citizen charter; the module should let each institution set its own target.

## 7. Authenticity and signature

- **Public checks.** Luxembourg's GouvCheck verifies any official document by
  its QR code in real time; India's e-Filing "Authenticate Notice/Order" and
  GST "Verify RFN" work without login; the SFIO prints both a QR code and a
  document number; Malaysia put QR codes on lockdown letters to stop forgery;
  HMRC's QR codes lead to guidance only. The repeated advice: reach the check
  through the issuer's own address, not a link in the letter.
- **Signature law.** Under eIDAS a qualified electronic signature has the
  legal effect of a handwritten one in every EU state (Article 25(2)), and
  public services may not demand more than qualified (Article 27). Turkey's
  KEP makes registered electronic mail legally equal to wet-signed paper.
  Indonesia's signatures come from BSrE under the national cyber agency.
  Russia's MEDO moves the signature with the document in one container.
- **Ethiopia.** The Electronic Transactions Proclamation 1205/2020 is the
  mandate MInT cites for e-government. Whether and how an electronic
  signature on an official letter replaces the stamp and wet signature is a
  decision for the owner with INSA, the certification authority; until then
  the module records approval and prints a signed paper with a verification
  code.

## 8. Ethiopian practice

The informal sources agree on the elements of an Ethiopian official letter:
the reference number (ቁጥር) and the date (ቀን) in the Ethiopian calendar at the
top, the sender's stamp, the addressee (ለ), the subject (ጉዳዩ) often citing the
legal instrument, the body, the signatory, and the copies (ግልባጭ) at the end.
Institutions keep an inward book (ገቢ) and an outward book (ወጪ), and the
registry desk stamps the inward number on the paper. No national e-office
system or correspondence standard is published; the owner's rules stand in:
Amharic and English letters are numbered separately, and numbering patterns
are the institution's own.

## 9. Gaps and recommendation

Already covered by the module after today's two cuts: both registers, numbering
per institution, direction and language, routing with instructions and
deadlines, inter-institution delivery with receipt, copies, classification,
file code, dispatch register with acknowledgement, verification code and public
check, reminders, audit, export, reports, bilingual templates with versions.

Applied on 2026-11-10 (third cut): the letterhead per institution with header
and footer (emblems, bilingual names, parent body, contacts in header or
footer, footer text, motto, page numbers, official stamp on dispatched
letters); the reply-time standard per institution in working days by priority
with default due dates, interim-reply requests and escalation to the
signatories; the file classification scheme per institution with a platform
fallback; and email dispatch to parties outside the platform with the PDF and
verification code, recorded in the dispatch register.

Still open, both by the owner's decision:

1. **Electronic signature**, once the owner and INSA decide the certificate
   source: the module already records who approved and when, and prints the
   official stamp.
2. **Retention schedules** from the National Archives and Library Agency, in
   the platform's retention matrix; **OCR** of scans for full-text search.

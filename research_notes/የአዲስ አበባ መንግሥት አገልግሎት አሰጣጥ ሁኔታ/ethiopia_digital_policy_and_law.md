## 1. Digital Ethiopia 2025, its successor Digital Ethiopia 2030, MInT e-government and the national e-services portal

### Takeaway
Digital Ethiopia 2025 has been succeeded by **Digital Ethiopia 2030 (ዲጂታል ኢትዮጵያ 2030, DE2030)**, a five-year strategy approved by the Council of Ministers in late November 2025 and launched by PM Abiy Ahmed on 20 December 2025. "Modernised government services" is one of its cross-cutting pillars. Specific commitments match the proposed platform almost one-to-one: integrated e-services, Fayda and data-exchange APIs, user-feedback and complaint mechanisms on every major platform, quarterly service-performance dashboards, digital signatures and paperless workflows, and civil-service digital capacity.

### Inferences
- A city platform with QR complaints and feedback, leadership dashboards, Fayda-based identity and APIs maps directly onto these DE2030 commitments: user-feedback and complaint mechanisms, quarterly performance dashboards, DPI and EthioConnect integration through APIs, Digital-by-Default, digital signatures and paperless workflows, and civil-service digital-readiness KPIs. Cite DE2030 rather than DE2025 as the current national strategy.
- DE2030 explicitly expects regional and city administrations to build their own digital roadmaps, delivery teams and reporting. A city project can present itself as Addis Ababa's contribution to that "whole-of-government" requirement.

### Gaps
- The final PMO-published DE2030 text could not be retrieved. Page numbers and KPI values come from the NBE-hosted draft (Dec 2025) and must be checked against the final version before quoting.
- No official Amharic title or Amharic text of DE2030 was retrieved beyond "ዲጂታል ኢትዮጵያ 2030".
- The "National Digital Government Strategy" named in DE2030 was not located. Its title, date and status are unverified.
- The national e-services portal URL and its current service count were not verified from the portal itself; "over 800" is DE2030's figure.

## 2. Fayda national digital ID: law, NIDP/FaydaVerse, enrolment, mandates, civil servants, Addis Ababa rollout

### Takeaway
The legal basis is the **Ethiopian Digital Identification Proclamation No. 1284/2023** (የኢትዮጵያ ዲጂታል መታወቂያ አዋጅ), confirmed from the gazette text. Under it, Fayda is "legal and sufficient evidence" of identity, refusing a valid digital ID is a crime, and any relying party that wants to make Fayda mandatory needs regulator approval and NIDP authorisation.

Enrolment figures, by date and source:

| As of | Registrations / users | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2026 | 39+ million | World Bank |
| May 2026 | 43 million | ID4Africa deck |
| Aug 2026 | about 50 million users | Kana TV |

On 4 Aug 2026 the programme moved into a state-owned enterprise, **FaydaVerse**. Sectoral mandates exist for banking, TINs, title deeds and education. No national directive requiring civil servants to register with Fayda was found.

### Cited Findings
- Gazette facts: Federal Negarit Gazette 29th Year No. 19, Addis Ababa, 18 April 2023 (ሚያዚያ ፱ ቀን ፪ሺ፲፭ ዓ.ም). The table of contents reads "Proclamation No. 1284/2023 Ethiopian Digital Identification Proclamation"; the Amharic title is "አዋጅ ቁጥር ፩ሺ፪፻፹፬/፪ሺ፲፭ የኢትዮጵያ ዲጂታል መታወቂያ አዋጅ". Article 26: in force on the date of publication. — [Proc. 1284/2023 gazette copy (metaappz)](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2023/pr_1284_2023.pdf)
- Key articles:
  - Art. 15(1): "Any Relying Party that wants to put Digital Identification as a mandatory requirement to provide service shall get approval from the concerned regulator."
  - Art. 15(4): Relying Parties "shall obtain authorization from the Institution before they start providing service".
  - Art. 15(5): if a Relying Party cannot obtain data from the Institution, it may serve "using their own other method".
  - Art. 15(6): data-retention periods for relying parties are set by NIDP directive.
  - Art. 16(1): "Digital Identification may be taken as a legal and sufficient evidence to proof or authenticate a Resident's identity."
  - Authentication providers must share "only minimal necessary level of personal data", and data received "can be used only for that particular service for which it was requested".
  - Art. 22(1): criminal liability for anyone who "refuses to receive digital identification as a legal identification or refuse[s] to provide services with Digital Identification".

  — [Proc. 1284/2023](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2023/pr_1284_2023.pdf)
- A law-firm summary states the proclamation prohibits collecting ethnic origin, blood group, DNA, religion, health record and criminal record. This was **not located in the extracted English text** and needs checking against the gazette. — [Dablo Law Firm, 12 Apr 2023](https://dablolawfirm.com/?p=6436)
- Implementer: the National ID Program (NIDP) under the Office of the Prime Minister. The World Bank Ethiopia Digital ID for Inclusion and Services Project (P179040; US$300m IDA + US$50m WHR grant; Board approval 13 Dec 2023) supports it. — [World Bank Aide Memoire, ISM 23–30 Mar 2026](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051826153515642/pdf/P179040-74a164c1-0bb2-49b6-8b5f-2da36395da02.pdf)
- **March 2026** (World Bank):
  - "Fayda registration has reached 39+ million as of March 2026, projected to grow to 60 million by June and 90 million by September 2026"
  - "More than 9 million Fayda cards have been issued and over 78 million authentication transactions recorded… daily volumes averaging 1.5–2 million"
  - "integrated entities has reached 100+ public and private institutions… including financial services and e-government"
  - "VeriFayda 2.0 is operational, offering secure e-signature and authentication services based on OpenID Connect and OAuth 2.0"
  - Fayda is getting ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification
  - NIDP "has been operationalized as an SOE (FaydaVerse)"

  — [World Bank Aide Memoire, Mar 2026](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051826153515642/pdf/P179040-74a164c1-0bb2-49b6-8b5f-2da36395da02.pdf)
- **May 2026** (NIDP presentation at ID4Africa 2026):
  - "43 M Registration; 120+ integrations; 102M Authentication; 2M/day; 12 M+ Printed Credentials; 10K Registration Kits"
  - Roadmap: 10M (2024), 70M (2026), 90M (2028)
  - Mandates and uses: "NBE mandated new and existing bank accounts to be harmonized in phases"; "Fayda has been made mandatory for NEW TINs nationally" (5M+ existing TINs harmonized); "Fayda is mandatory for notary & title-deed services"; "Fayda is mandatory for transfer, sales and renewal of title-deeds"; "AA [Addis Ababa] Civil Registration and Vital Events Agency" listed among basic-service integrations; national grade-12 and exit-exam registration
  - Mesob is described as a "Fayda based digital one-stop shop for e-government services"
  - Integration portal named "id.et/connect"

  — [NIDP, ID4Africa 2026 deck, May 2026](https://id4africa.com/2026/files/T1-2-1-Ethiopia-FINAL.pdf)
- **August 2026** (Kana TV): "FaydaVerse began operating on August 4, 2026, taking over management of Ethiopia's national identity system. The entity is a state-owned enterprise under Ethiopian Investment Holdings"; authorized capital 10 billion Birr. The system "has processed 190 million secure authentications, connected more than 165 public and private institutions, issued over 15 million physical ID cards, and deployed more than 12,000 registration kits"; the article describes "fifty million users". It reports that NBE mandated every bank account be linked to Fayda by December 2026, that school registration requires it "since this year", and that the 90-million target date has shifted between 2026, 2027, 2028 and 2030 across official documents. — [Kana TV, Aug 2026](https://kanatelevision.com/news/what-is-faydaverse-the-ethiopian-national-id-program-that-became-a-company-and-how-it-plans-to/)
- Earlier baseline (draft DE2030): "By mid-2025, over 23 million people were enrolled in Fayda and integrated with 55+ service providers". The same draft elsewhere says "over 25 million… used across 55 services", an internal inconsistency. — [Draft DE2030](https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Draft_Digital_Ethiopia_2030.pdf)
- Addis Ababa rollout: the city "has halted the collection of biometric data for its residential ID card following the integration of the city's registration system with the Fayda National Digital ID". The residency card now incorporates the Fayda registration number and has been rolled out in "nearly 89 districts". "Since transitioning from paper-based IDs to digital in 2021, 2.8 million residents have obtained digital IDs" (Addis Ababa Civil Registration and Residency Service Agency). **Article date not visible**. — [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-ababa-integrates-fayda-national-digital-id-into-residency-cards)
- The NIDP executive director, Yodahe Zemichael, was reported as saying the e-ID would be made obligatory for access to public services, given low public uptake. **Date not captured.** — [Acteurs Publics (French)](https://acteurspublics.fr/articles/lethiopie-va-rendre-lidentite-numerique-obligatoire-pour-acceder-aux-services-publics/)
- Mesob Regulation 583/2026, Art. 17, lists "National identification No" as step 2 of the Mesob service-delivery process map. — [Reg. 583/2026](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2026/re_583_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- A city employee registry and ID card that stores or verifies the Fayda number makes the City Administration a "Relying Party". It needs NIDP/FaydaVerse authorisation (Art. 15(4)), must keep the minimal-data and purpose limits, and must offer an alternative route if Fayda data cannot be obtained (Art. 15(5)). Making Fayda mandatory for employees or service users needs approval from the "concerned regulator" (Art. 15(1)).
- VeriFayda 2.0 (OIDC/OAuth 2.0) is the technically aligned integration path for authenticating employees and citizens.
- Because the city residency card already embeds the Fayda number, seeding Fayda numbers into the employee registry is consistent with existing city practice.

### Gaps
- No national or Addis Ababa directive was found that requires civil servants to register with Fayda or links payroll to Fayda. One search snippet asserted "the digital ID is mandatory for civil servants and students", but its source could not be identified: **unverified**.
- No Addis-Ababa-specific Fayda enrolment total from an official source was found.
- The identity of "the concerned regulator" for approving mandatory use under Art. 15(1) was not established.

## 3. Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024: obligations for public bodies

### Takeaway
**Proclamation No. 1321/2024** (የግል ዳታ ጥበቃ አዋጅ) was published 24 July 2024 and took effect on publication. The regulator is the **Ethiopian Communications Authority (ECA)**. The proclamation binds government bodies, and these duties apply directly to a city HR and complaints platform:
- lawful bases including legal obligation and public-authority functions
- data minimisation and storage limitation
- a mandatory DPO for government bodies
- registration with ECA
- DPIA for risky processing
- 72-hour breach notification to ECA and to data subjects
- in-country storage
- portability and automated-decision rights

### Cited Findings
- Gazette: 30th Year No. 35, Addis Ababa, 24 July 2024 (ሐምሌ ፲፯ ቀን ፪ሺ፲፮ ዓ.ም); "Proclamation No. 1321/2024 Personal Data Protection Proclamation"; Amharic "አዋጅ ቁጥር ፩ሺ፫፻፳፩/፪ሺ፲፮ የግል ዳታ ጥበቃ አዋጅ". Art. 70: "This proclamation shall enter into force on the date of publication in the Negarit Gazeta", signed 24 July 2024 by President Sahle-Work Zewde. — [Proc. 1321/2024 gazette copy](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Parliament adopted it on 4 April 2024. — [Digital Policy Alert (via search)](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/24905-adopted-personal-data-protection-proclamation-proclamation-no-13212024-including-cybersecurity-regulation)
- Regulator: Art. 2(36) "'Authority' means the Ethiopian Communications Authority established as per the Communications Proclamation No. 1148/2019". This matches DE2030: the PDP "established the Ethiopian Communications Authority as the regulator". — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf); [Draft DE2030 p. 14](https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Draft_Digital_Ethiopia_2030.pdf)
- In March 2026 the World Bank listed as an ongoing action (owners NIDP/ECA): "Continue support for operationalization of the Data Protection Commission and alignment with the Digital ID ecosystem". This suggests a dedicated data-protection body is being set up; it is not reconciled with the ECA designation in the law. — [World Bank Aide Memoire, Mar 2026](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099051826153515642/pdf/P179040-74a164c1-0bb2-49b6-8b5f-2da36395da02.pdf)
- Principles (Art. 6): processed lawfully, fairly and transparently; specified purposes; and "kept… for no longer than is necessary for the purposes" (Art. 6(5)). — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Lawful bases (Art. 7(2)) include:
  - (c) "necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject"
  - (e) "necessary… to fulfill functions of public authority which necessarily includes the processing of personal data for the fulfillment of its mandate within the limits of a law issued for this purpose"

  A separate condition covers "the lawful and non-commercial objectives of public organizations". — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Data sovereignty (Art. 22(1)): "Every data controller or data processor shall ensure the storage, on a server or data center located in Ethiopia, of personal data collected or obtained locally". Under Art. 22(2) ECA may designate "critical personal data" that may only be processed in Ethiopia. Cross-border transfer conditions are in Arts. 20–21. — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Data subject rights include information (controllers must disclose DPO contact, lawful basis, recipients, "retention periods"), access, rectification and erasure, objection, Art. 31 automated individual decision-making, and Art. 32 portability "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format". — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf); summary in [CIPIT](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/?p=4868)
- Registration (Arts. 33–35): controllers and processors register with the Authority, which issues "a certificate of Registration which is valid for a period of two years; the certificate shall be renewed every two years" (Art. 35(2)). — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- DPO (Art. 40(1)(a)): a controller or processor "shall designate… a data protection officer… where: a) The processing is carried out by a goverment body, except for courts". One DPO may serve several public bodies. Contact details must be published and sent to the Authority (Art. 40(5)). — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Breach notification: Art. 43(1) requires notice to the Authority "within 72 hours after having become aware of it"; Art. 44(1) requires the controller to tell the data subject "within 72 hours". — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Art. 47: a data protection impact assessment is required before processing that "may result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects". Records of processing are also required, and the Authority sets log-retention periods. — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)
- Penalties: offences committed by an institution, involving sensitive data, or concerning minors can bring "a fine up to four per cent of its total worldwide turnover". Transfers abroad in violation and the sale of personal data carry 5–10 years' imprisonment. Art. 69 empowers Council of Ministers regulations and Authority directives. — [Proc. 1321/2024](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2024/pr_1321_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- For an Addis Ababa platform holding employee records, biometrics or photos, QR complaint data and service-eligibility data:
  - appoint a DPO (mandatory for government bodies)
  - register with ECA (two-year certificate)
  - run a DPIA, especially for biometric or NFC ID cards and cross-bureau data sharing
  - host in Ethiopia
  - build 72-hour breach workflows
  - define and publish retention periods
  - rely on Art. 7(2)(c)/(e) (legal obligation / public-authority functions) rather than consent for core HR processing
- Public complainants submitting via QR are data subjects too. Anonymous or minimal-data options support the minimisation principle.

### Gaps
- No ECA directives or Council of Ministers regulations implementing 1321/2024 were found. Registration fees, forms and the critical-data list are unknown.
- The status of the "Data Protection Commission" mentioned by the World Bank is unclear.

## 4. Electronic transactions, e-signature, computer crime, and INSA security requirements

### Takeaway
**Electronic Transaction Proclamation No. 1205/2020** expressly covers the Addis Ababa City Administration. It obliges public bodies to accept and issue electronic documents and to roll out e-services, while not forcing anyone to use electronic means (Art. 4).

E-signatures are governed by **Proclamation No. 1072/2018**, amended by **No. 1358/2024**, which gives INSA and the Council of Ministers rule-making power. **Computer Crime Proclamation No. 958/2016** remains the criminal-law base.

INSA (now "Information Network Security Administration") audits critical systems. The new **Critical Infrastructure Cybersecurity Proclamation No. 1426/2026** takes effect 21 July 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Proclamation 1205/2020: Federal Negarit Gazette 26th Year No. 57, 30 June 2020; "Electronic Transaction Proclamation No. 1205/2020". Its preamble: "deploying electronic government service in support of good governance is essential for building effective, accountable and inclusive institutions at all levels". — [Proc. 1205/2020 gazette copy](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2020/pr_1205_2020.pdf)
- Art. 3(1)(a): it applies "on institutions of the Federal and Regional Governments, and the City Administration of Addis Ababa and Diredawa which have the power and function to provide services to the public". — [Proc. 1205/2020](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2020/pr_1205_2020.pdf)
- Art. 4 "Use not Mandatory": "Nothing shall make it mandatory for a person to use, provide or accept any electronic message in any commercial transaction or dealings with government bodies unless the person consents". — [Proc. 1205/2020](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2020/pr_1205_2020.pdf)
- Art. 35(1) ("Acceptance of Electronic Filing and Issuing of Documents"): any government agency that accepts filings, keeps records, or issues permits, licences or approvals "shall… accept the filing of such documents… in the form of electronic messages; issue such permit, license or approval in the form of an electronic message or make or receive payment in electronic form". ## 5. Civil service law: federal proclamation, Regulation 201/2018, Addis Ababa city law, the responsible bodies

### Takeaway
Federal level:
- The federal civil service law in force is the **Federal Civil Servants Proclamation No. 1353/2025** (የፌደራል መንግሥት ሠራተኞች አዋጅ ቁጥር 1353/2017), published 9 Jan 2025. It repealed No. 1064/2017.
- It creates a Merit and Wage Board, puts **one-stop digital public service** into law (Arts. 44–45), and obliges every government office to feed a national civil-servant database and build an **HR management information system** (Arts. 89–91).
- The responsible federal body is the **(Federal) Civil Service Commission**, established by Proclamation 1263/2021, Art. 42.

Addis Ababa:
- **Regulation No. 201/2018** is an **Addis Ababa City Administration** regulation, not a federal one. It covers civil-servant discipline, grievance and appeal procedure and is in force from 16 Jan 2026.
- It was issued by the City Cabinet under the **Addis Ababa City Administration Civil Servants Proclamation No. 87/2017 (E.C.)** and gives the city **Public Service and Human Resource Development Bureau** power to issue implementing directives.

## 6. Mesob (መሶብ) one-stop service centre

### Takeaway
Mesob was unveiled by PM Abiy in Addis Ababa in April 2025; ENA gives 26 April 2025 as the operational date. The PM framed it as an answer to public anger over "land issues and corruption". Its legal basis is Proc. 1353/2025 Art. 44(6) and **Council of Ministers Regulation No. 583/2026**, which establishes the "One-Stop Shop Digital Public Service" (MESOB Service) as an independent federal office accountable to the Federal Civil Service Commission.

The regulation mandates real-time dashboards, once-only reuse of customer data, data protection, support to city administrations, and a complaint unit that decides within two working days. Addis Ababa's own "Addis Mesob" centres were reported in six sub-cities, with the remaining five planned. Fayda is part of the Mesob process.

### Inferences
- The city platform's QR complaint flow, dashboards and once-only data reuse mirror duties that Reg. 583/2026 places on the MESOB Service: real-time dashboards on response time, complaints and satisfaction; reuse of customer data; a technology-supported complaint system with a two-working-day decision; and support to city administrations. The project can be positioned as the back-office and employee-accountability layer behind Addis Mesob centres.
- Mesob's anti-corruption framing (PM, Mayor, Proc. 1353 Art. 44(1) "getting rid of wrongdoing and corruption") can be cited directly.

### Gaps
- The exact date and the current count of Addis Ababa sub-city Mesob centres are unconfirmed. The Fortune article's date is not visible.
- No Addis Ababa city legal instrument establishing Addis Mesob was found.
- Service and centre counts conflict across sources because they come from different dates and definitions.

## 7. National public-service reform programmes, service standards, Ten-Year Plan and Homegrown Economic Reform

### Takeaway
Current reform framing:
- A **Public Service and Management Policy (2024)** with seven pillars, one being digitising the civil service. This comes from UNDP, secondary.
- Proc. 1353/2025's merit, competency and performance provisions.
- Mesob as the flagship service-delivery reform.
- DE2030, which cites the Homegrown Economic Reform Agenda and DE2025 as its foundations.

The Ten-Year Development Plan (2021–2030) is reported to include an "efficient civil service" pillar and a digital-economy pillar, but its text was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- UNDP (9 Oct 2025): the government "initiated a Public Service and Management Policy (2024) structured around seven key reform pillars". According to the Federal Civil Service Commissioner, the sixth is "digitising and digitalising the civil service"; others include service improvement and modernisation, and modern human resource management. The article reported a study visit to Kenya's Huduma model. — [UNDP, 9 Oct 2025](https://www.undp.org/node/549176) (content from search-result excerpt; page returned 403 to the fetcher, so this is partially verified)
- ENA (8 Jul 2026): the Commission prioritises institution-building, administrative restructuring, merit-based recruitment and a more inclusive, diverse workforce. — [allAfrica/ENA, 8 Jul 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202607090662.html)
- Proc. 1353/2025's preamble and provisions: competency-based recruitment; a competency assurance and evaluation system; "Reinforcing the System of Performance" (Art. 46); one-stop digital public service (Arts. 44–45). — [Proc. 1353/2025](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2025/pr_1353_2025.pdf)
- DE2030: "The Homegrown Economic Reform Agendas and Digital Ethiopia 2025 have opened key sectors, laying fertile ground for innovation". It also says change management "is a transformation of how government operates, how services are delivered, and how institutions interact with citizens". — [Draft DE2030, pp. 2–3, ~85](https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Draft_Digital_Ethiopia_2030.pdf)
- Ten-Year Development Plan (2021–2030), "A Pathway to Prosperity": a secondary summary lists ten strategic pillars, including "access to justice and efficient civil services" and "technological capability and digital economy". — [Effective Cooperation (GPEDC) summary, via search; page 403](https://effectivecooperation.org/ethiopias-national-development-vision-and-plans) (**unverified**)
- Service standards and citizen charters: Reg. 583/2026 Art. 16 requires a service catalogue with "Service delivery standard such as time, expense, quantity and quality", customer preconditions and delivery locations. Art. 8(9) requires standardised service-delivery procedures. — [Reg. 583/2026](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2026/re_583_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Defensible alignment chain for the proposal (የፖሊሲ ትስስር):
  1. Ten-Year Plan (efficient civil service, digital economy)
  2. HGER
  3. DE2030 (modernised government services, DPI, feedback, dashboards)
  4. Public Service and Management Policy 2024 (digitisation pillar)
  5. Proc. 1353/2025 (Arts. 44–45, 89–91)
  6. Reg. 583/2026 (Mesob standards, dashboards, complaints)
  7. Addis Ababa Proc. 87/2017 E.C. and Reg. 201/2018 (city HR law)

### Gaps
- The text of the "Public Service and Management Policy (2024)" was not retrieved, so its official title (Amharic and English), adoption date and pillar wording are **unverified**.
- The Ten-Year Development Plan text was not retrieved. Pillar names come from a secondary summary.
- No national "citizen charter" instrument or "public service delivery policy" document was located.

## 8. National and regional HRMIS and unified civil-servant ID initiatives

### Takeaway
Proc. 1353/2025 now makes a national civil-servant database and office-level HRMIS a legal obligation (Arts. 89–91). Earlier and regional efforts exist:
- a Civil Service Commission digitisation project with HR, tribunal, HR-audit, organogram and archive modules (around 2019–2020)
- an INSA-built HRMS for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (2020)
- a reported 96% digitisation of Amhara personnel records

No national unified civil-servant ID card programme was found.

### Cited Findings
- Proc. 1353/2025 Arts. 89–91 (see Q5): a national civil-servant database run by the Commission's Information Service in cooperation with regional and city governments; offices must send real-time employee profiles and "Organize, develop and implement the human resource management information system". — [Proc. 1353/2025](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2025/pr_1353_2025.pdf)
- Positions carry identification numbers recorded in a database (job-grading provisions). No provision on an employee identification card was found in the English text. — [Proc. 1353/2025](https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2025/pr_1353_2025.pdf)
- Addis Fortune ("Radar", dated Sep 2019; page also shows 2020) reported that the Civil Service Commission's services were digitised by local firm Custor Computing at 50 million Br. There are five modules: "Human Resources, Tribunal, HR Audit, Organogram and Archives". The Commission oversaw 195 federal offices and 1.7 million civil servants. — [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/civil-service-commission-digitises-services) (from search-result excerpt; not opened)
- INSA launched a Human Resources Management System for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (9 Dec 2020). — [Ethiopian Embassy](https://ethiopianembassy.org/insa-launches-hrms-for-foreign-affairs-ministry-december-9-2020/) (from search result)
- Amhara Region reports having "digitized over 96% of personnel records for regional government officials and civil servants" and is rolling out Mesob-style hubs. — [Amhara Media Corporation](https://www.ameco.et/english/?p=2805) (search-result excerpt; page returned HTTP 500; date unknown, **unverified**)
- DE2030 KPIs set targets for public servants trained in digital skills (40% by 2027, 75% by 2030) and a "Civil service digital readiness index". — [Draft DE2030 p. 91](https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Draft_Digital_Ethiopia_2030.pdf)

### Inferences
- An Addis Ababa employee registry with real-time data and APIs would be the city-level counterpart that Art. 89 expects the federal Information Service to "cooperate" with. The proposal can frame its data-exchange APIs as a means of meeting Art. 91-style real-time reporting.
- A unified employee ID card with QR/NFC has no specific national legal template. It is best justified through Fayda integration (Proc. 1284/2023), PDP compliance (Proc. 1321/2024), and the HRMIS and service-accountability provisions above.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) official announcement of a national civil-service HRMIS rollout, or of its name, was found.
- No national or Addis Ababa initiative for a unified civil-servant ID card (QR/NFC) was found.
- No information was found on whether the Addis Ababa PSHRD Bureau operates an HRMIS today.

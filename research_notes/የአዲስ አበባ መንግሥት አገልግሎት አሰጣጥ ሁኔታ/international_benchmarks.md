# International Benchmarks and Good Practice: Technology-Enabled Public Service Delivery and Civil Service Management (for Addis Ababa proposal: "lessons from other countries" / የሌሎች ሀገሮች ተሞክሮ and "standards")

Research date: 2026-10-26. All figures carry the year they refer to. "Self-reported" marks figures from the programme operator, its government sponsor or friendly media rather than an independent evaluation. Where sources conflict, both are given.

## 1. Rwanda: Irembo (citizen e-services) and IPPIS (civil-service HR/payroll)

### Takeaway
Irembo shows that a single government e-service portal with assisted channels (agents, USSD) can triple volumes and raise officer productivity ~3.4x without adding staff. Rwanda's own HR/payroll system (IPPIS), which has the same modules the Addis project plans (discipline, grievance, leave, career, recruitment), was criticised by Parliament in 2021: after 9 years only 5 of 11 modules worked. The likely cause was governance and phasing, not technology.

### Cited Findings
**Irembo: scale and results (mostly self-reported)**
- IremboGov offers **248 services from nearly 40 institutions** (Irembo impact story, published 9 Jul 2025; self-reported). — [Irembo, 2025](https://irembo.com/2025/07/irembogov-scaling-adoption-and-delivering-a-better-experience-for-civil-status-services/)
- Civil-status certificates (birth, single, marriage) delivered rose from **683,560 in 2020 to 1,977,628 in 2024** ("almost tripled"). — [Irembo, 2025](https://irembo.com/2025/07/irembogov-scaling-adoption-and-delivering-a-better-experience-for-civil-status-services/)
- Share of these applications issued **within one hour: 61% (2020) → 70% (2024)**. — [Irembo, 2025](https://irembo.com/2025/07/irembogov-scaling-adoption-and-delivering-a-better-experience-for-civil-status-services/)
- **Applications handled per officer: ~905 (2020) → 3,047 (2024), i.e. 3.4x**, while officer headcount "remained largely constant". This came from workflow automation and removing physical queues. — [Irembo, 2025](https://irembo.com/2025/07/irembogov-scaling-adoption-and-delivering-a-better-experience-for-civil-status-services/)
- Over **5 million service requests processed for MINALOC (Ministry of Local Government) in 2023–2024**, according to a minister's quote. — [Irembo, 2025](https://irembo.com/2025/07/irembogov-scaling-adoption-and-delivering-a-better-experience-for-civil-status-services/)
- Stated enablers for inclusion and adoption:
  - a nationwide **agent network** that helps citizens with low digital literacy;
  - **USSD access** for feature phones;
  - the **"Byikorere" awareness campaign**;
  - a mobile-first **IremboApp**;
  - **instant payment** through the citizen's preferred channel.
  — [Irembo, 2025](https://irembo.com/2025/07/irembogov-scaling-adoption-and-delivering-a-better-experience-for-civil-status-services/)
- A 2026 University of Rwanda news item reports **more than 240 services** and, citing RISA (Rwanda Information Society Authority), **more than 3 million users per year**. — [University of Rwanda / Salus, 2026](https://salus.ur.ac.rw/news/59/irembo-revolutionizes-access-to-government-services-in-rwanda)
- At the 2020 relaunch (IremboGov 2.0), the platform was reported to have **saved applicants ~23 million hours** of in-person application time. This is self-reported. — [KT Press, Feb 2020](https://www.ktpress.rw/2020/02/rwanda-launches-a-more-user-friendly-online-portal-for-govt-services/)

**Irembo: governance model**
- Irembo describes itself as a technology company founded in Kigali in **2014**. Its first product, IremboGov, launched in **July 2015 "in partnership with the Government of Rwanda"**, and it now calls itself the government's "Digital Services Partner". — [Irembo About](https://irembo.com/about/)
- A UN DESA case study says the partnership grew out of an agreement between **RwandaOnline Platform Ltd and the Government**, a public–private partnership. — [UN DESA case page](https://publicadministration.desa.un.org/ru/node/1600)
- KT Press (2022) names Irembo Ltd as the government's partner. — [KT Press, Jun 2022](https://www.ktpress.rw/2022/06/wtdc-2022-how-digitalisation-transformed-service-delivery-in-rwanda/)
- RISA is described as a key partner in the upgraded IremboGov platform. — [New Times press release](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/23594/news/featured/press-release-service-migration-to-upgraded-irembogov-begins-paving-way-for-faster-improved-service-delivery/amp)

**IPPIS (Integrated Personnel and Payroll Information System): problems recorded by Parliament (Oct 2021)**
- IPPIS was **initiated in 2012**. It was to deliver **11 modules by December 2020**:
  - payroll;
  - organisation structure and contract management;
  - career development;
  - **discipline management**;
  - exit and termination;
  - **grievances**;
  - leave and absence;
  - results-based management;
  - e-recruitment;
  - talent and training.
  — [KT Press, Oct 2021](https://www.ktpress.rw/2021/10/ministry-of-labor-pinned-over-loopholes-in-personnel-payroll-system/)
- Parliament's Public Accounts Committee (PAC) found **only 5 modules developed (45%)**. **Six were non-functional**: career development, discipline, grievance, leave and absence, talent and training. The cost exceeded **Rwf 2.6 billion**. — [KT Press, Oct 2021](https://www.ktpress.rw/2021/10/ministry-of-labor-pinned-over-loopholes-in-personnel-payroll-system/)
- Following Auditor-General queries, the PAC also reported:
  - **no user manual and no process documentation**;
  - **no change control board**, so changes were made without approval;
  - **no record of the donor funding** (World Bank, DFID) spent since 2010;
  - **123 other government projects not integrated** with IPPIS.
  — [KT Press, Oct 2021](https://www.ktpress.rw/2021/10/ministry-of-labor-pinned-over-loopholes-in-personnel-payroll-system/)
- One MP asked why IPPIS was not "put in phases since it was evident that it couldn't be implemented in a single phase". — [KT Press, Oct 2021](https://www.ktpress.rw/2021/10/ministry-of-labor-pinned-over-loopholes-in-personnel-payroll-system/)
- Benefits acknowledged in the same debate:
  - civil servants receive **salaries on time**;
  - candidates can view applications and **file appeals online in e-recruitment**, which reduces corruption in hiring.
  — [KT Press, Oct 2021](https://www.ktpress.rw/2021/10/ministry-of-labor-pinned-over-loopholes-in-personnel-payroll-system/)
- MIFOTRA (Ministry of Public Service and Labour) states that IPPIS e-recruitment is now the channel for recruiting public servants. It told visiting delegations that IPPIS helped eliminate pay fraud and ghost workers. These are self-reported claims and the page is undated. — [MIFOTRA](https://www.mifotra.gov.rw/news-detail/recruitment-of-public-servants-digitized-in-rwanda); [MIFOTRA (Gambia study tour)](https://www.mifotra.gov.rw/news-detail/mifotra-hosts-a-delegation-from-gambia-on-a-study-tour-in-rwanda)

### Inferences
- For Addis Ababa, the strongest Irembo lesson is the operating model: one front door; assisted digital channels for people who cannot self-serve; payment integration; and measuring throughput per officer. Raising productivity with flat headcount is a better efficiency argument than claims of "hours saved".
- IPPIS is a direct cautionary case for the proposed HR modules. Phase the delivery, keep process documentation and user manuals from day one, set up a change control board, and track donor and own-budget spending per module.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-Irembo, non-government) evaluation of Irembo's time and cost savings was found.
- A KT Press award article reports ">1.5 billion working hours saved", which is far above the 2020 figure of ~23 million hours. It is undated and I could not pin down its URL, so treat it as unreliable.
- PPP contract terms were not found: concession length, revenue share, and ownership or exit of the platform and data.
- No current (2023–2026) status of IPPIS module completion was found.

## 2. Kenya: Huduma Centres, eCitizen, and HR/payroll unification (IPPD → HRIS-Ke, Unified Payroll Number)

### Takeaway
Kenya combines physical one-stop shops (Huduma, ~14 million visits a year) with a single online payment and service portal (eCitizen, ~21,000 services claimed). Demand for in-person service stays high even after digitisation. Kenya's new unified HR/payroll system (HRIS-Ke) was found "erroneous" by the Auditor-General in 2025. The causes were weak governance, poor data migration from the legacy system, configuration gaps and security gaps, while the system still carried over 17,000–19,000 ghost workers.

### Cited Findings
**Huduma Centres (one-stop shops)**
- Physical visits reached **14,121,167 in FY2024/25, up from 13.5 million in FY2023/24** (National Treasury data). Business Daily headlines this as "Kenyans stick to in-person services". — [Business Daily, 2025](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/huduma-centre-visits-hit-14-1m-kenyans-stick-in-person-services-5326544)
- Huduma's CEO reports an average of **~60,000 customers served daily**. The FY2025/26 target is at least 14 million. — [Huduma Kenya weekly bulletin, Jan 2026](https://www.hudumakenya.go.ke/weekhiidocs/2026january20260123.pdf); [Kenya News Agency](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/huduma-kenya-to-advance-service-delivery-to-citizens/)
- Academic studies of Nairobi centres (small samples, not national) report:
  - dissatisfaction with **waiting time, operating hours and complaint handling**;
  - **system breakdowns, counter delays and understaffing**;
  - that centres **"lack customer feedback mechanisms to evaluate performance"**.
  
  They recommend citizen feedback and separating self-service, quick-service and long-service zones. — [Africa Nazarene University repository](https://repository.anu.ac.ke/handle/123456789/306)
- A 2016 study at the GPO (General Post Office) Huduma centre (132 customers) found customers broadly content. Recurring complaints were system outages, weak communication and poor customer care. — [Daystar University repository](https://repository.daystar.ac.ke/handle/123456789/5521)

**eCitizen (single online portal and payment gateway)**
- Services on eCitizen grew **from ~350–397 in 2022 to ~20,855–22,000 in 2024–2025**. Officials give different numbers, all self-reported:
  - Immigration PS Bitok: 354 → 18,000 (late 2023). — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/julius%20bitok/90872/kenya-collects-sh400bn-in-revenue-with-18-000-services-on-e-citizen--ps-bitok)
  - Prime CS Mudavadi: ~22,000 (Nov 2024). — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/musalia-mudavadi-e-citizen-services-have-cut-down-on-corruption-in-govt-n353749)
  - PS Kipsang: 397 (2022) → more than 21,000 (Aug 2025). — [Business Daily / Mwanaspoti](https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/corporate/boss-talk/belio-kipsang-on-e-citizen-growth-transparency-concerns-and-what-the-future-holds-for-digital-service-delivery-5171082)
- Government spokesperson: monthly revenue collected through government digital channels rose from **KSh 1.45 billion (2022) to KSh 10.9 billion (2024)**, +652%. This is self-reported. — [Capital FM, 2024](https://capitalfm.africa/govt-revenue-up-to-sh10-9bn-monthly-in-2024-from-sh1-45bn-in-2022/)
- Revenue figures conflict:
  - PS Bitok cites ~**KSh 400 billion** in FY2023/24, of which KSh 300bn came through e-Citizen. — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/julius%20bitok/90872/kenya-collects-sh400bn-in-revenue-with-18-000-services-on-e-citizen--ps-bitok)
  - CS Mbadi told the Senate the revenue was **KSh 100.8 billion** in FY2023/24. — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/digital%20government%20services/79439/mbadi-says-e-citizen-tripled-its-revenue-to-sh100-billion-in-one-year)
  - The sources do not reconcile the two.
- In Aug 2025 the government publicly "affirmed full control over e-Citizen platform". This signals a platform-control and vendor dispute; I saw the headline only. — [The Star, 17 Aug 2025](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2025-08-17-state-affirms-full-control-over-e-citizen-platform)

**HR/payroll unification: IPPD → HRIS-Ke and the Unified Payroll Number (UPN)**
- A Public Service Commission (PSC) report found **19,467 ghost workers in national government in FY ending June 2023**. Cabinet then approved a unified personnel identification system. — [Nation, 2024](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/inside-government-plan-to-purge-ghost-workers-from-civil-service-payroll-4793268)
- PS Gathecha (Apr 2024) said every civil servant would get a **Unified Payroll Number (UPN)**. The UPN was piloted with the Teachers Service Commission, and **540,523 payroll numbers were generated between Jan 2020 and Mar 2024**. — [Kenyans.co.ke, Apr 2024](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/99713-govt-process-overhauling-civil-servants-payroll-system)
- A later PSC report found **more than 17,000 ghost workers still being paid** across agencies. — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/corruption%20ghost%20workers/105682/state-agencies-still-pay-17-000-ghost-workers-psc-report-shows)
- The **Auditor-General's special audit of HRIS-Ke** was tabled in Parliament on **19 Jun 2025** and reported on 4 Aug 2025. It found the system "erroneous", with:
  - **weak governance, incomplete functionalities and glaring security gaps**;
  - **data-migration discrepancies** from IPPD: employees present in one system but missing from the other, and unjustified job-group changes;
  - **retirement-age employees still on payroll**;
  - **no pre-payroll audits** and no previous-month payroll reports;
  - **over- and under-payment of allowances**, because the system did not enforce Salaries and Remuneration Commission rules;
  - **county designations** (ward administrators, revenue clerks) **not configured**; counties joined in Dec 2024.
  
  The Auditor-General recommended:
  - an ICT strategy, budgets and **formal service agreements**;
  - **NDAs with developers**;
  - a **quality-assurance team**;
  - integration with **KRA (tax authority) and NSSF (social security fund)**.
  — [The Star, 4 Aug 2025](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2025-08-04-new-state-payroll-system-erroneous-report)
- The World Bank tied about KSh 94 billion in loans to payroll reforms. The reforms include harmonising HR and payroll data, introducing unified payroll numbers and cleaning county payrolls, based on Auditor-General findings. The article date was not captured. — [Education News Kenya](https://educationnews.co.ke/?p=527367)

**Ethiopian analogue**
- Ethiopian officials credit "digital transactions and the **Mesob service**" (Ethiopia's one-stop service centres) with reducing corruption and fraud. This is a self-reported claim (ENA, Dec 2025). — [ENA via AllAfrica, Dec 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512240227.html)

### Inferences
- Lesson for Addis Ababa: digital channels do not remove the need for well-run physical service points. Kenya's in-person volume kept growing alongside eCitizen. QR-code feedback at physical service points targets exactly the weakness that Kenyan studies found: no feedback mechanism, and poor complaint handling.
- A unified employee number and master registry only removes ghost workers if four conditions hold: migration is reconciled record by record, retirement and exit rules are enforced in the system, the registry is integrated with tax, pension and payroll, and governance (QA team, change control, contracts) is in place before go-live.
- Platform control matters. Formal service agreements and government ownership of the platform and data should be in the contract from the start (see the eCitizen control dispute and the Auditor-General's NDA recommendation).

### Gaps
- No independent national evaluation of Huduma Centres was found (e.g., satisfaction index or wait-time trend).
- No audited figures on eCitizen time and cost savings were found.
- HRIS-Ke rollout status after the June 2025 audit, and actual ghost-worker removals attributable to the UPN, were not found.
- Details of the eCitizen vendor-control dispute were not verified beyond the headline.

## 3. Estonia: X-Road data exchange, "once-only", and digital ID

### Takeaway
Estonia's X-Road is open-source, decentralised data-exchange middleware. It carries ~2.2 billion transactions a year among ~78,000 organisations, with signed, encrypted and logged messages. It is the reference design for the secure API and data-exchange layer of the Addis project. Time-savings figures are widely quoted but inconsistent, so cite them with caution.

### Cited Findings
- X-Road serves **78,000 organisations as indirect users**, handles **2.2 billion transactions per year**, and offers **over 3,800 e-services** (e-Estonia, current page). — [e-Estonia X-Road](https://e-estonia.com/x-road/)
- Security design: **"all outgoing data is digitally signed and encrypted, and all incoming data is authenticated and logged"**. X-Road can write to multiple systems and query several at once. Estonia and Finland federated their X-Road instances in **Feb 2018**. — [e-Estonia X-Road](https://e-estonia.com/solutions/interoperability-services/x-road/)
- X-Road is **open source with no licensing fees** for low- and middle-income countries. Adopters "**avoid a vendor lock-in situation**" and get support from a global community. Evidence of each exchange is **stored locally by the exchanging parties**, with no third-party access. — [X-Road Global (NIIS)](https://x-road.global/the-17-goals)
- Time saved (inconsistent estimates):
  - **3,225 working years in 2014**, when X-Road handled ~113 million queries (GEN Global profile, secondary). — [GEN Global](https://www.genglobal.org/atlas/e-estonia)
  - **"3 million working hours annually"** (slide in a 2024 Oregon legislative committee document, secondary). — [Oregon Legislature, 2024](https://apps.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Downloads/CommitteeMeetingDocument/280098)
  - A widely quoted figure of **"1,345 working years saved per year"** is attributed to e-Estonia and X-Road sites but did not appear on the current pages I fetched. Do not cite it without the original.
- Digital ID:
  - **99% of Estonian residents have the ID-card**;
  - e-Estonia claims **5 days per year saved per person through digital signatures**;
  - the card is used for digital signatures, i-Voting, e-Prescriptions, health insurance and tax claims.
  
  These are self-reported. — [e-Estonia ID-card](https://e-estonia.com/solutions/e-identity/id-card/)
- Estonian officials estimate digital-signature savings at **~2% of GDP per year**. This is a government estimate, not an audit, and one source notes the savings are hard to quantify. — [Communications of the ACM](https://cacm.acm.org/news/estonia); [RBC Thought Leadership](https://www.rbc.com/en/thought-leadership/innovation/estonia-may-be-the-worlds-first-digital-state-heres-what-we-can-learn-from-it/)

### Inferences
- Design principles transferable to Addis Ababa's API layer:
  - **Decentralised exchange.** Each agency keeps its own data, so the city does not build one central database.
  - **Message-level signing, encryption and logging** for every exchange between agencies.
  - **Open standards and open source** to avoid vendor lock-in.
  - **Reuse of a national ID as the citizen and employee key.** In Ethiopia's case this is Fayda (Proclamation 1284/2023; see section 6).
- "Once-only" means citizens and employees should not have to resubmit data the government already holds. It follows from data exchange plus a single identifier. Staff service eligibility (cafeteria, transport) should be computed from the master registry rather than re-collected.

### Gaps
- No primary methodology was found for any X-Road time-saved figure.
- The legal basis for Estonia's once-only rule (e.g., the Public Information Act) was not verified in this session.

## 4. Other relevant examples with measured outcomes

### Takeaway
India's CPGRAMS (Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System) cut average grievance resolution time from 28 days (2019) to 13 days (2024) through process reform, not software alone. Pakistan's Punjab Citizen Feedback Monitoring Program (CFMP) shows that proactively asking citizens about a just-completed service produces actionable data. India's iGOT Karmayogi links mandatory staff learning to performance appraisal. Ghana's payroll clean-ups show measurable fiscal savings from validating the employee registry.

### Cited Findings
**India: CPGRAMS (public grievance platform)**
- **Average grievance redress time fell from 28 days (2019) to 13 days (2024)**. More than **1.12 crore (11.2 million) grievances were resolved between Jan 2020 and Oct 2024**, with an annual high of **23.24 lakh**, and **more than 1 lakh grievance officers** are mapped. The government credits a **10-step CPGRAMS reform** adopted in 2022. — [All India Radio News, Dec 2024 (Lok Sabha reply)](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/more-than-1-12-cr-public-grievances-resolved-during-period-of-jan-2020-to-oct-2024-on-cpgrams-portal)
- The DARPG (Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances) monthly report puts the Jan–Nov 2024 average across all ministries at **13 days**. — [PIB (Press Information Bureau) report, Dec 2024](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/dec/doc20241218473901.pdf)
- In **Aug 2024** the maximum redress time was cut **from 30 to 21 days**, with interim replies required for longer cases (comprehensive guidelines, Aug 2024). — [Upstox News, Aug 2024](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/centre-reduces-public-grievance-redressal-time-from-30-days-to-21-days-all-you-need-to-know/article-112902/)

**India: iGOT Karmayogi (civil-service learning platform)**
- iGOT Karmayogi crossed **1 crore (10 million) registered civil servants in May 2025**. — [All India Radio News, 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/igot-karmayogi-platform-crosses-mark-of-1-cr-registered-civil-servants)
- A **4 Jul 2025 memo** made prescribed annual courses mandatory for central government and All India Services officers. Completion is recorded in their **Annual Performance Appraisal Reports (APARs)**. Later aggregator figures (Jul 2026) cite over 1.69 crore users and over 5,500 courses, but these could not be checked against the primary record. — [SuperKalam summary of parliamentary answer, Jul 2026](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/24-07-2026/parliament-question-mission-karmayogi-7340ca93-f251-4f0a-b7c4-d749b4a39d1d)

**Pakistan (Punjab): Citizen Feedback Monitoring Program (proactive SMS and call feedback after a service)**
- The programme had **contacted 23 million citizens since 2012** (by 2018) and logged **39,000 actions by district officials** (May 2018 release). — [PITB (Punjab Information Technology Board) CFMP press release, May 2018](https://www.pitb.gov.pk/system/files/PITB%20CFMP%20May%202018.pdf)
- PITB attributes a rise in medicine dispensation at district and tehsil hospitals (DHQ/THQ) **from 46% (Oct 2015) to 83% (Jun 2017)** to corrective actions based on CFMP feedback. This is self-reported. — [PITB](https://pitb.gov.pk/node/1129)
- A Princeton ISS (Innovations for Successful Societies) case study (2008–2014) found the call-centre data helped district coordination officers identify poorly performing employees and offices. The model spread to other provinces and federal agencies. — [Princeton ISS](https://successfulsocieties.princeton.edu/publications/calling-citizens-improving-state-pakistan%E2%80%99s-citizen-feedback-monitoring-program-2008-%E2%80%93)
- Caveat: the Global Delivery Initiative (GDI) case study was written by a PITB director, so it is not independent. No rigorous impact evaluation was found. — [Effective Cooperation / GDI](https://www.effectivecooperation.org/system/files/2021-06/GDI%20Case%20Study%20Improving%20Service%20Delivery%20through%20Citizen%20Feedback%20in%20Pakistan.pdf)

**UK: "Tell Us Once" (once-only notification)**
- One report of a death automatically notifies HMRC (tax), DWP (benefits and pensions), the Passport Office, DVLA (driving licences), the local council, Veterans UK and public-sector pension schemes. — [GOV.UK Tell Us Once](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/organisations-you-need-to-contact-and-tell-us-once)

**Ghana: payroll and employee-registry validation**
- More than **67,000 ghost names have been removed** from the public payroll, attributed to biometric verification, data reconciliation and monitoring (Deputy Finance Minister, Apr 2026). — [Ecofin Agency, Apr 2026](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news/2804-55066-ghana-payroll-purge-removes-67-000-ghost-names-government-says-it-frees-fiscal-space)
- OSP (Office of the Special Prosecutor) and CAGD (Controller and Accountant-General's Department): **GH¢34.25 million saved in 2024** by blocking payments to deceased, retired and untraceable persons. The probe found that **deceased and retired validators' credentials were still being used** to validate payroll. — [Starr FM, May 2024](https://starrfm.com.gh/2024/05/osp-cagd-have-saved-ghana-ghc34m-from-ghost-names-on-govt-payroll); [Modern Ghana](https://www.modernghana.com/news/1313643/osp-cagd-identifies-clears-ghost-names-on-gove.amp)
- A Fair Wages and Salaries Commission audit of 17 institutions saved **~GH¢36.1 million between Apr and Aug 2023**. — [Ghanaian Times, 2023](https://ghanaiantimes.com.gh/ghana-saves-over-gh%c2%a236m-after-payroll-audit-rollout/)
- The Controller-General (Jul 2025) said deceased staff, long-term absentees and former employees still appear on the payroll because of **lapses in the Electronic Salary Payment Voucher validation process**. — [AllAfrica, Jul 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202507290240.html)

### Inferences
- CPGRAMS is the best evidence for the grievance and complaint module. Its gains came from fixed redress deadlines (30 → 21 days), named grievance officers, interim replies and monitoring, with the software as the enabler. These can be written directly as service standards for Addis Ababa's QR-feedback and complaint workflow.
- Punjab CFMP supports *proactive* feedback, i.e. asking the citizen after the service. This complements the passive QR-code feedback planned for Addis; both should feed leadership dashboards.
- Ghana shows that the identity of the people who validate records (approver credentials) and offboarding of leavers matter as much as the employee registry itself.

### Gaps
- No measured savings figures were fetched for UK Tell Us Once.
- No independent evaluation of iGOT's effect on performance was found.
- No Singapore or Morocco case was researched, for time reasons.
- No published QR-code-at-counter feedback programme with measured outcomes was found.

## 5. Global assessments placing Ethiopia and Addis Ababa

### Takeaway
On the UN E-Government Survey 2024, Ethiopia ranks 169th of 193 (EGDI 0.3111), well below the Africa average (0.4247) and the world average (0.6382). Addis Ababa's city portal scores 0.3333 on the UN Local Online Service Index (LOSI) 2024 (rank 103). The World Bank GovTech Maturity Index (GTMI) 2022 reportedly moved Ethiopia from Group C to Group B, which indicates core government systems are stronger than citizen-facing online services.

### Cited Findings
**UN E-Government Survey 2024 (UN DESA, published Sep 2024)**
- Ethiopia **EGDI 2024 = 0.31109, rank 169/193**, up from **0.28650 and rank 179 in 2022**. It is in the Middle EGDI group, rating class M1. — [UN DESA country profile: Ethiopia](https://publicadministration.un.org/egovkb/en-us/Data/Country-Information/id/58-Ethiopia)

## 6. Recognised standards and frameworks a professional proposal can cite

### Takeaway
A credible proposal can anchor its sections on five groups of standards:
- service design: the UK GDS Service Standard;
- information security: ISO/IEC 27001;
- complaints handling: ISO 10002;
- quality management: ISO 9001;
- business case: the HM Treasury Five Case Model.

Results logic (logframe or results-based management), delivery methods (PRINCE2 or PMBOK, ITIL for operations) and Ethiopia's own data-protection and digital-ID law complete the set.

### Cited Findings
**Service design and delivery**
- **UK Government Digital Service (GDS) Service Standard: 14 points.** They include:
  1. understand users and their needs;
  2. solve a whole problem for users;
  3. provide a joined-up experience across all channels;
  4. make the service simple to use;
  5. make sure everyone can use the service;
  6. have a multidisciplinary team;
  7. use agile ways of working;
  8. iterate and improve frequently;
  9. create a secure service that protects users' privacy;
  
  and points on open standards, choosing the right technology, measuring performance and reliable operation. — [GOV.UK Service Manual: Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard) (fetched 2026-10-26)
- **OECD Recommendation of the Council on Digital Government Strategies (2014)**, the OECD's legal instrument on digital government. — [OECD Legal Instruments, OECD-LEGAL-0406](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0406) (not fetched this session)
- **World Bank ID4D Principles on Identification** for digital ID design: inclusion, design and governance. — [World Bank ID4D Principles](https://id4d.worldbank.org/principles) (not fetched this session)

**Business case and results frameworks**
- **HM Treasury Five Case Model** (Green Book business-case guidance):
  - the five cases are strategic, economic, commercial, financial and management;
  - business cases go through three stages: Strategic Outline, Outline and Full;
  - it is required for UK centrally funded spending proposals;
  - the guidance claims **30–40% savings in the time and cost of producing business cases** compared with unstructured approaches.
  — [HM Treasury, Guide to Developing the Programme Business Case (2018)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6644948aae748c43d3793bb9/Programme_Business_Case_2018.pdf); [The Green Book (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book-appraisal-and-evaluation-in-central-government/the-green-book-2020)
- New Zealand Treasury states the **G20 adopted the Five Case Model in 2018** as an international standard for infrastructure projects. — [NZ Treasury Better Business Cases](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/information-and-services/state-sector-leadership/investment-management/better-business-cases)
- **Results-based management and logframe**: the UN Development Group's *Results-Based Management Handbook* (2011) sets out the results chain (inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact) used in government and donor projects. — [UNSDG RBM Handbook](https://unsdg.un.org/resources/results-based-management-handbook) (not fetched this session)

**Management-system standards (ISO)**

The iso.org pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-10-26. The editions below are those in the ISO catalogue at the URLs given.
- **ISO 10002:2018**, *Quality management: Customer satisfaction: Guidelines for complaints handling in organizations*. Use it for the QR complaint and feedback workflow (acknowledgement, tracking, response time, analysis). — [ISO 10002:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/71580.html)
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**, *Information security management systems: Requirements*. Use it for the employee registry, ID credentials and APIs. — [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)
- **ISO 9001:2015**, *Quality management systems: Requirements*, for documented processes and continual improvement. — [ISO 9001:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/62085.html)

**Ethiopian legal standards the system must comply with**
- **Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024**:
  - adopted **4 Apr 2024**, in force **24 Jul 2024**;
  - sets data-subject rights and controller and processor duties;
  - requires **local storage** of personal data collected in Ethiopia.
  — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/24905-adopted-personal-data-protection-proclamation-proclamation-no-13212024-including-cybersecurity-regulation); [CIPIT (Strathmore University) analysis](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/?p=4868)
- **Digital ID Proclamation No. 1284/2023** established the national digital ID (**Fayda**). Fayda issues a 12-digit identifier and is designed around data minimisation and privacy by design. — [CIPIT analysis](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/?p=4868); [UNECA, Digital ID in Ethiopia](https://www.uneca.org/sites/default/files/TCND/Digital%20ID%20_in_Ethiopia.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested mapping of standards to proposal sections:

  | Proposal section | Standard or framework |

## 7. Common failure factors of large government HRMIS and e-government projects in Africa

### Takeaway
The recurring failure pattern has six parts:
- "big-bang" scope that outruns capacity (Rwanda IPPIS);
- dirty data and unreconciled migration (Kenya HRIS-Ke);
- partial module rollout that leaves manual workarounds (Malawi);
- systems not integrated with other HR, payroll and civil-registration data (Tanzania);
- weak governance and contracts with vendors (Kenya);
- designs copied from high-income contexts that do not fit local reality (Heeks).

Technology alone did not remove ghost workers anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Heeks' "design–reality gap" model: e-government projects in Africa "mainly end in partial or total failure" because designs from Western contexts do not match African public-sector realities. — [Heeks 2002, Information Polity](https://content.iospress.com/articles/information-polity/ip000008); [GSDRC summary](https://gsdrc.org/document-library/causes-of-e-government-success-and-failure-design-reality-gap-model/)
- Heeks (2003): "Most eGovernment-for-Development Projects Fail". Secondary sources cite his estimate as **~35% total failures, ~50% partial failures and ~15% successes** (based on about 40 projects). The year attributions vary between 2002 and 2006 across citing papers. — [Heeks 2003, iGov WP14 (Univ. of Manchester)](https://www.gdi.manchester.ac.uk/research/publications/working-paper-archive/igov/igov-wp14/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/Abstract=3540052); secondary: [Danubius University journal](https://journals.univ-danubius.ro/index.php/oeconomica/article/download/1778/1476)
- **Rwanda IPPIS** (2021):
  - scope of 11 modules at once, with 6 still non-functional after 9 years;
  - no documentation and no change control;
  - untracked donor money.
  — [KT Press, Oct 2021](https://www.ktpress.rw/2021/10/ministry-of-labor-pinned-over-loopholes-in-personnel-payroll-system/)
- **Kenya HRIS-Ke** (2025):
  - migration discrepancies;
  - unenforced business rules (retirement, allowances);
  - configuration gaps;
  - security gaps;
  - missing pre-payroll audit;
  - weak governance and contracts, so the Auditor-General asked for an ICT strategy, formal service agreements, developer NDAs and a QA team.
  — [The Star, Aug 2025](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2025-08-04-new-state-payroll-system-erroneous-report)
- **Malawi**: of about 10 HRMIS modules, **only 3 (payroll, establishment, employee data) were operational**. Losses were linked to manual manipulation of payroll forms, ghost workers and unauthorised codes in the HRMIS (National Audit Office). — [The Nation (Malawi)](https://mwnation.com/ghosts-terrorise-capital-hill-coffers/)
- **Tanzania**: HCMIS (Human Capital Management Information System) "is not a panacea" for ghost workers; results depend on ethical and professional bureaucrats. A dissertation found ghost workers persisted because the system was not integrated with other HR systems, so it failed to catch deceased and retired staff. — [Kampala Bible University journal (AJOGPL)](https://ajogpl.kab.ac.ug/index.php/1/article/view/40); [Mzumbe University journal](https://ujmdd.mzumbe.ac.tz/index.php/ujmdd/article/view/189)
- **Ghana**: credentials of deceased and retired validators were still being used to validate payroll. The C&AG (Controller and Accountant-General) blamed validation lapses in 2025. — [Starr FM, May 2024](https://starrfm.com.gh/2024/05/osp-cagd-have-saved-ghana-ghc34m-from-ghost-names-on-govt-payroll); [AllAfrica, Jul 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202507290240.html)
- **Kenya Huduma**: system breakdowns, understaffing and no feedback mechanism undermined satisfaction. — [ANU repository](https://repository.anu.ac.ke/handle/123456789/306)
- **Vendor lock-in**: X-Road's open-source model is promoted specifically to "avoid a vendor lock-in situation". — [X-Road Global](https://x-road.global/the-17-goals)
- **World Bank guidance (Cambodia technical note, Dec 2024)**: it recommends formal adoption of a **phased HRMIS modernisation roadmap** with short-, medium- and long-term modules. Piloting a payroll data-entry module showed quality and efficiency gains at moderate cost. — [World Bank OKR](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/3ecc3e73-561f-4d2d-9072-ff3f80962b32)

### Inferences
Mitigations the Addis proposal can state explicitly, each traceable to a failure above:
1. **Phased rollout.** Start with the master registry and ID, then self-service, then the HR case modules. Set a go/no-go gate per phase (Rwanda IPPIS; World Bank Cambodia).
2. **Data cleansing and record-by-record reconciliation before migration**, including a headcount or biometric validation exercise (Kenya; Ghana).
3. **Business rules enforced in the system**: retirement, exit and eligibility for cafeteria and transport benefits (Kenya).
4. **Integration** with payroll, pension, tax and Fayda through APIs (Tanzania; Estonia).
5. **Governance**:
   - a steering committee and change control board;
   - an independent QA team;
   - formal SLAs (service-level agreements);
   - government ownership of source code and data;
   - escrow and open standards against lock-in (Rwanda; Kenya; X-Road).
6. **Change management and assisted channels for low-literacy users**, plus staffing of service points (Irembo agents; Huduma).
7. **Measure outcomes, not outputs**: turnaround time, first-contact resolution, complaint closure within SLA, and productivity per officer (CPGRAMS; Irembo).

### Gaps
- No single World Bank cross-country HRMIS failure study (2020–2026) with quantified failure rates was found. The Heeks figures date from 2002–2003 and were verified only through secondary citations.
- Sustainability evidence (operating budgets and post-donor funding of HRMIS in Africa) was not found and remains a gap.
